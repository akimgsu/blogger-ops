10. IAM and SSO

When companies grow to dozens of internal systems, per-app accounts become an operational and security nightmare. IAM centralizes identity; SSO turns one login into many app sessions.

What IAM Covers

  • Identification of users and service accounts
  • Authentication, including MFA and passkeys
  • Authorization via roles, groups, and policies
  • Provisioning across joiners/movers/leavers
  • Audit trails for who accessed what, when

Without IAM, password policies diverge, terminated employees linger in fringe apps, and audits fail.

How SSO Works

Users authenticate once at a central IdP. Trusted applications (SAML SPs or OIDC relying parties) consume that authentication instead of prompting again.

[User] -> [App A] --redirect--> [IdP Login]
[User] -> [App B] --redirect--> [IdP already signed in] -> SSO success

Remember the split: the IdP session is central, while each app may still create its own local session. Single Logout must account for both layers.

Business and Security Value

  • Less password fatigue and fewer reset tickets
  • Central MFA enforcement
  • Disable once at the IdP to cut off many apps
  • Centralized audit logs

Trade-off: the IdP becomes a critical dependency and prime attack target—high availability and hardening are non-negotiable.

Chapter Summary

  • IAM manages identity and access across the full lifecycle.
  • SSO reuses one IdP authentication across trusted apps.
  • OIDC and SAML are the dominant protocols.
  • Protect and scale the IdP; separate IdP sessions from app sessions.
